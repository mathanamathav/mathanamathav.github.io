Hi, I'm Mathan.

I grew up in Madurai and went to school there, then spent five years in Coimbatore doing an integrated M.Sc. in Data Science at [PSG College of Technology](https://www.psgtech.edu/).

So far I've had two stints in industry. The first was an internship in my third year, working as a software developer at a startup called [ZeroDown](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/zerodown). These days I'm a founding engineer at [DataGenie](https://www.datagenie.ai/) — a small, collaborative team where I'm constantly learning. It's unconventional, full of interesting people with very different perspectives, and there's always something new to figure out.

Outside of work, I just like building and tinkering — that's how I started, actually. My first real project was a [social network](https://github.com/mathanamathav/socialnetwork), basically a mini Facebook clone. I picked it up alongside my coding buddy [Ashish](https://github.com/Ashishkumaraswamy), learning HTML, CSS, and JavaScript from the famous CS50 course just to figure out how to put it together — and we went on to build plenty of other interesting projects together. I still remember the late nights getting the chat to work, then the rush of deploying it and nudging my friends to sign up and see it live. Another favourite was [Bunker](https://github.com/mathanamathav/bunker-api), a college project I built with [Partha](https://github.com/Partha16) and [Sukumar](https://github.com/sukumar1612) for those of us who liked to skip class whenever we could — some juniors still find it useful and have carried it forward.

It's a strange, exciting time to be a software developer. It's easy to get overwhelmed, but I think that AI and the new coding tools make building far more accessible than before. To me they're less a crutch and more a personal assistant — Jarvis to Iron Man. Coding agents like Claude are genuinely reliable for building interesting things; the rest is just time and your own curiosity. They're enablers.

I'm building this site to document what I work on, so that in a few years I can look back and see how my thinking — and I — have changed over time. In the meantime, I'll keep building projects and taking part in hackathons to sharpen my skills and help with the things I work on day to day.
